Belmont Lake State Park

Pollinator Pathway

This garden was established in fall of 2024 to showcase the beauty of plants that are native to the Northeastern US. The native plants you see here are adapted to the local soil, require little maintenance, no fertilizer and seldom need watering.

In addition, they provide invaluable ecosystem services, such as food and habitat for wildlife as well as the preservation of our natural resources like clean air and water. Using a large assortment of plant species in the garden increases both biodiversity and seasonal interest.

54 species were included to promote environmentally healthy landscaping practices and to inspire park visitors to add ecologically beneficial plants into their home landscapes. This garden was designed and installed by the Bethpage State Park Horticulture Program.

The pathway runs the length of the garden, west to east, with the border on one side and the corner bed and woodland garden on the other. Every bed is keyed by the code painted on it — tap one to open the plant. Gold means it should be in bloom this month, and searching below dims the beds that do not match.
